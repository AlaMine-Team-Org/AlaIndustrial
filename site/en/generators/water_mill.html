Water Mill

alaindustrial:water_mill
GeneratorsLVImplementedtier-lvenergywater

Summary

The Water Mill turns on flowing water and runs around the clock — night and storms mean nothing to it. No fuel, no tanks, no pumps: give it a current past the wheel and it produces EU/FE for as long as the water keeps moving.

Mini-example: a channel with a waterfall on either side of the wheel → 4 EU/FE/t, non-stop. Over one in-game day that is 96 000 EU/FE — eight times what a T1 solar panel makes in the same time (12 000 EU/FE, and only by day).

The one catch: still water does not count. Dropping the wheel into a pond or a river is not enough — it needs a real current.

Energy

The mill only produces power. Output leaves through the back face, which carries the connector; the other five faces are inert for energy.

Generation

The wheel hangs one cell in front of the block, and water is counted around the wheel, not around the mill. The four working cells mirror how real water wheels are driven:

CellWhat it is called
Below the wheelundershot — the classic; the rim dips into the current
Above the wheelovershot — water poured over the top
Left and rightthe horizontal sweep of the rim

Each cell with a current gives 1 EU/FE/t, all four give the maximum 4 EU/FE/t. The hub cell (directly in front of the mill) does not count: that is where the axle sits.

Only flowing or falling water counts. Rivers and oceans are made of source blocks, so "put it in a river" will not work. The reliable trick: place a source one cell above the wheel's cell — falling water never becomes a source. Pouring a bucket straight into the wheel's cell is useless, because that cell then becomes a source itself.

Room for the wheel

The wheel is large (rim about 2.7 blocks) and needs room to turn. All nine cells of the wheel's plane are checked — the front column and both neighbours, each on three levels, bottom row and diagonals included. A solid block in any of them hides the wheel and stops generation: the screen reads "Blocked". Water and passable blocks (grass, flowers, snow) do not obstruct it, so a channel around the wheel works fine. The check runs every tick: remove the block and the wheel is back.

Several mills together

If two wheels overlap in space, both mills stop with the status "Wheel interference".

ArrangementGap needed
Side by side2 empty blocks
Face to face2 empty blocks
Back to backflush is fine

A neighbour without a wheel causes no trouble — but put a wheel in it and the working mill beside it stops too.

Inventory

One slot for the water wheel and four upgrade slots. Without a wheel the mill produces nothing and the external model is not drawn.

The wheel is a consumable. It only wears while the mill is actually producing EU/FE (no current or an obstruction means no wear) and in proportion to output. A full wheel is worth 320 000 EU/FE: about an hour of non-stop work at maximum, or 6–7 in-game days at a typical 2 EU/FE/t. When it runs out the wheel breaks with a sound and generation stops until a new one is fitted.

Interface

Energy bar on the left, wheel slot in the centre, indicator on the right: a white wheel means idle, blue means working. The line under the slot shows either the current output or the reason it stopped: "Wheel interference", "Blocked" or "No water". While no wheel is fitted the line is empty — the empty slot says it already.

Visual

A large open wheel turns in front of the mill: two 24-segment oak rims, eight spokes on each side, twelve buckets between the rims, a wooden hub and a copper axle. The stronger the current, the faster it spins; with no current the wheel stands still.

Sound

While the wheel turns you hear a quiet splash of water within about 12 blocks. It falls silent when the wheel stops.

Recipes

Crafted at a workbench from planks, copper ingots, a copper cable and a wooden gear. The water wheel is crafted separately — the mill does not work without it.

Related

Solar Panel — the other passive source, but it depends on the sky and the time of day. Generator — twice the output, but it needs fuel. Water Wheel — the consumable the mill cannot run without.
