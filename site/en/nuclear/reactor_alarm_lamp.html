Alarm Lamp

alaindustrial:reactor_alarm_lamp
NuclearImplementednuclearreactoralarmredstonelight

Summary

The Alarm Lamp is a red beacon in an iron cage. Two things light it, and either one is enough:

  1. The reactor it is pressed to. A lamp placed with its base on the shell of a formed reactor room — on the roof, on a wall from the outside or from the inside — or on the controller or a rack of a bare reactor, becomes part of that reactor and lights on its own by the reactor's alarm: no dust, no antenna. You can put up as many lamps as you like (owner's decision, 2026-11-04).
  2. Redstone, anywhere. From the reactor antenna's boom over any circuit of yours — dust, repeaters, an inverter — at the other end of the base, by your bed, at the entrance; and just as well from a pressure plate by a door or a mob farm: the lamp does not care where the signal came from.

It goes on any of the six faces: base against the block you place it on, cage pointing away from it.

The lamp makes no sound (the alarm sound belongs to the future reactor siren, and does not tell signal strengths apart: any signal above zero lights it, and from its reactor it is lit from the warning all the way up to the emergency.

A reactor with an antenna and dust and a lamp pressed to the reactor are two independent paths: the lamp still works from redstone, and it is lit while at least one of them is on.

A known caveat: a controller destroyed by an explosion (rather than by a player) leaves the shell "formed" until the next build — the same cosmetic loose end as the shell's seamless texture — and a lamp on such a shell may stay lit. A player who breaks the controller takes the room apart, and the lamps go dark.

Telling a warning from an emergency

The antenna boom gives 7 on a warning and 15 in an emergency (see the antenna). The lamp lights on either of them, and a circuit tells them apart:

  • Dust length. The signal drops by 1 for every block of dust. A lamp more than 7 blocks of dust away from the boom only gets a signal from 15 — so it lights only in an emergency; a nearer one lights already on the warning.
  • A comparator in subtract or compare mode separates 7 from 15 just like any other signal.

A home-made emergency stop

The controller runs while a signal reaches it. Lead the alarm signal through an inverter (a redstone torch) to the controller, and the reactor stops itself on an alarm. Without the inverter the alarm signal would do the opposite and start the reactor — which is why the boom's tooltip and the guide book say so plainly. The boom itself never touches the controller and never feeds it a signal (see the antenna).

Light and flashing

  • The light of a lit lamp is a steady 14, as bright as a torch (owner's decision, 2026-11-04). Only the picture flashes: the block's light does not jump, or the world would recalculate its lighting on every flash.
  • The flashing is an animated lens texture (.mcmeta): a running highlight no more than once a second, even, with no random drop-outs (the same rule as for the tablet screen in.
  • The lens is drawn with light_emission: 15 in the model, so the flash reads in complete darkness.
  • The animation runs on the atlas's shared clock: every lamp in the world flashes in sync. That is expected — vanilla animated blocks flash the same way.
  • A lamp that is off has a dark red lens with no glow and looks exactly like a lamp with no signal.

The lamp is a metal light fitting, not a wall block of the reactor: it needs none of the shell's toughness. It does not take a place in the room's wall and does not count as a breach — it is pressed to the shell from the outside or the inside.

Appearance

The look is variant 01 "Beacon in a cage", chosen by the owner on 2026-11-03 out of three sketches (tools/model_sources/reactor_alarm_lamp_concepts/, source v01_cage_beacon/reactor_alarm_lamp.bbmodel): a red glass lens in an iron cage on a dark base, with a cap on top.

  • Two models: off (a dark red lens) and on (a lens with the animated texture and light_emission: 15), turned by the block state to each of the six faces, like a vanilla end rod.
  • The item icon is the off model.
  • The cage does not hide its neighbours: the block is not an occluder, so neighbouring faces are drawn.

It reads like the device itself: a vanilla lamp in an iron housing behind red glass, plus a wire. The recipe is not tied to the nuclear stage — the lamp suits any circuit and is available right away.
