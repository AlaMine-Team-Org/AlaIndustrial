Incubator

alaindustrial:incubator
AgricultureLVImplementedtier-lvmultiblockmutationuraniumagriculturerarity

Summary

The Incubator is the mod's first multiblock (after the teleporter) and its first agriculture machine. You place the base block — a squat mechanical unit with an emitter ring, cooling ports and a spinning rotor — and put any glass (the c:glass tag) on top. The pair assembles into a multiblock: the glass turns into a volumetric rounded dome, a translucent incubation chamber with the irradiated item floating and spinning inside. Not "two stacked cubes": the silhouette reads as a dome, with frame ribs and a glow rising from below. Hybrid cost + probabilistic outcome. An operation always spends EU/FE (LV) and an irradiation charge: one uranium_ingot is taken the moment it lands in the slot of an assembled machine (dome on; no chip or input required — the charge pips fill before your eyes) and provides a charge of 3 attempts; once the charge is spent, the ingot burns out into, which settles in a separate ash slot (the 1 ingot → 1 depleted material balance is preserved). The mutation itself is probabilistic — success depends on the mutation kind (duplicate / transform / create) and can be overridden per recipe. On failure the input item is lost while the charge is still spent — that is the balance lever against a "free diamond farm". The mutation chip sets the mode. One item may have several mutation recipes (a diamond can both duplicate and turn into an irradiated diamond), so the mode is chosen by an item in a dedicated chip slot — modelled on the solar panel and its alignment chips. Three chips — duplication / transformation / creation; whichever sits in the slot decides how the machine works. With no chip the Incubator does not run at all. The chip is not consumed it is a permanent module, not fuel; swapping it resets the operation in progress. Slag — the consolation outcome. A small share of attempts (5 %) ends not in nothing but in irradiated slag: the input is still lost, but the matter does not vanish without trace. Groundwork for the future — slag will become an input for reprocessing in later phases. Outcome rarity (the "card pack" model). Every mutation success rolls a second die — the outcome grade: Common (70 %) / Rare (20 %) / Epic (8 %) / Legendary (2 %). The grade is the item's "gene": a colored name, a lore line and a bonus to its further mutation chances — groundwork for the future nuclear and agriculture phases.

Tool enhancement (affixes) is NOT part of this iteration. A fourth operation kind, enhance — upgrading powered items with Capacious / Efficient / Overclocked bonuses — is deferred to the second iteration. Reason: its scope (attributes, per-item injection points, a separate balance pass) rivals the Incubator itself. The MVP architecture leaves room for it: the MutationKind enum and the chip system are designed with a fourth value in mind.

Energy

Hybrid model: EU/FE from the grid (LV, like the other machines) plus uranium spent as the irradiation fuel. Uranium is not destroyed — it becomes depleted_uranium, so material balance is preserved and spent uranium accumulates as a feedstock for the future nuclear phase (see nuclear/index).

FieldValue
Internal buffer8000 EU/FE (incubatorBuffer) — holds the costliest create operation in full
Max input32 EU/FE/tick (LV)
EU/FE/tick8 EU/FE/tick while running (incubatorEuPerTick) — four times the machine standard: irradiation costs more than grinding
Fuel1 uranium_ingot = a 3-attempt charge (taken the moment it is inserted into an assembled machine); after the 3rd attempt → 1 depleted_uranium into the ash slot

An LV machine: up to 32 EU/FE per tick, nothing extra is pulled from the grid, nothing explodes. Without uranium in the fuel slot an operation will not start (EU/FE is spent only while a cycle runs).

Processing

Operation duration is tied to the mutation kind, not to the block. EU/FE cost = duration × incubatorEuPerTick (8 EU/FE/tick). The Incubator is the mod's most energy-hungry LV machine: a routine macerator/furnace operation costs 200–300 EU/FE, here the floor is 2400 EU/FE and create runs at 8000 EU/FE. That is deliberate — irradiating matter should not cost the same as sawing a plank.

Three possible outcomes. The operation outcome is rolled first:

OutcomeProbabilityWhat happens
Successchance by class/kind (+ gene bonus, capped at 0.95)the result goes to the output slot + a grade roll
Slag5 p.p. (mutationSlagChance)the input is lost, drops into the output
Empty failurethe remainderthe input is lost, the output is unchanged

Slag is subtracted from the failure, never from the success: the success chance stays as it is, while some of the misses now yield at least something. If success + 5 % would exceed 1.0, the slag share is trimmed to the remainder. No grade is rolled for slag — slag is always Common. The rule applies to all three modes. Slag can drop from any mutation — duplicate, transform or create alike; the chance is the same (mutationSlagChance), with no per-mode overrides. Slag also cuts through the gentle failure of the upper create tiers: the input normally comes back, but 5 % of attempts still turn it into slag.

A fourth kind — enhance (tool enhancement) — is reserved for the second iteration; it is neither specified nor implemented here.

On success the result goes to the output slot; on failure the output slot is unchanged. The roll is made at the end of the cycle, not at its start, and that matters: until the last tick the machine does not know the outcome, so it can neither be read from the outside (through the block's lit state) nor discarded by pulling the input and putting it back. An outcome already rolled is held until the output slot has room, and it survives a chunk unload — waiting never turns into a second chance. The seed is not persisted: over the long run the expected value converges on the stated chances. The second roll — the outcome grade. Every successful operation rolls a rarity grade: Common 70 % / Rare 20 % / Epic 8 % / Legendary 2 % (level.random as well, not persisted). A Common grade leaves no trace on the item — no component is written, so stacking with vanilla items is preserved. Rare and above writes the mutation_grade component and the vanilla minecraft:rarity (name color: yellow / aqua / light purple). The input gene burns into a bonus. The input item's grade grants its chance bonus (+5/+10/+15 p.p., the total chance is capped at 0.95 — there are no guarantees) and burns away: the result always gets a fresh grade roll. A Legendary cannot be multiplied via duplicate — only spent for the higher chance (anti-ratchet). Both copies of a duplicate share one common grade roll per operation. Output-slot rule: an operation will not start if its result cannot merge with what already sits in the output slot — and items of different grades do not stack. This is the standard machine behaviour (canOutput), not a bug: take out the rare result and the line keeps running.

Five slots: the mode chip, fuel (uranium), the item to mutate, the result, and ash (spent uranium). The result and ash slots are read-only for the player; automation into the ash slot is pull-only.

Interface

The Incubator screen shows: EU/FE storage, cycle progress, the irradiation charge (0–3), the five slots (chip/fuel/input/result/ash), and a formed indicator (dome present on top) — without it an operation will not run even at full fuel and EU/FE. The screen is 14 pixels taller than the machine standard (176×180): the extra height is the clear band between the slot rows where the status line is printed. The energy bar runs down the left edge, and the irradiation charge is a vertical column of pips right of the fuel slot that fills from the bottom up, like a level gauge. Top row: chip, input, progress arrow, result; bottom row: fuel and ash. The chip slot is the top left one; while it is empty the status line names what the machine is missing ("Needs glass on top" → "Needs a chip"). The installed chip determines the mode, and the status line shows its name. Swapping the chip resets the progress of the current operation. The mode is not stored as a separate field — it is derived from the item in the slot, so it persists together with the inventory. Both halves of the multiblock are clickable. Right-clicking the dome opens the same menu as clicking the base: the dome is the half a player sees and reaches for, while the base is often sunk into the floor or walled in by hoppers.

Block Properties

A two-tier multiblock with deliberately different silhouettes: a heavy mechanical bottom and a light rounded top. "Two cubes stacked on top of each other" is explicitly not the goal. Lower tier — the base. A full 16³ cube (needed for collision, automation and the machine standard), but visually dense: a recessed front panel with indicators, an emitter ring along the top face, cooling ports on the sides. While an operation runs, the adds a spinning ring above the top face and an emitter glow — the volume comes from rendering on top of the cube, without breaking collision. Upper tier — the dome. The player places any glass (the c:glass tag) and, as the structure forms, it is replaced by the mod block with its own volumetric rounded model: an octagonal chamber tapering upwards, with frame ribs and a spire. The original glass type is remembered in the base's block entity, so: dismantling the multiblock returns exactly the player's glass (nothing is lost); stained glass tints the dome — 17 chamber colors without a single extra recipe: the 16 stained-glass dyes plus an uncolored variant (plain and tinted glass carry no dye, so a dome built from either stays uncolored); only the glass faces take the color, the frame and the spire always stay the casing's dark metal; the player never crafts a separate dome block — entering the mechanic stays simple.

Recipes

Crafting the base block follows the machine-assembly standard and fills the whole 3×3 grid: 1 machine casing + 2 uranium plates + 3 glass + 1 electronic circuit + 2 iron plates. The profile part is uranium plates (the irradiation theme): entering the mechanic is gated behind an established uranium chain (iron pickaxe → ore → ingot → plate). Crafting the mutation chips. The three chips are made from (already in the mod) plus a thematic filling, modelled on the alignment chips. Cost rises with the "complexity" of the mode:

ChipRecipeRole
mutation_chip_duplicateempty chip + 2 amethyst shards + uranium plateduplication mode (amethyst grows by itself — the doubling theme)
mutation_chip_transformempty chip + redstone + lapis + uranium platetransformation mode (two "opposite" minerals — the exchange theme)
mutation_chip_createempty chip + diamond + echo shard + uranium platecreation mode (the costliest — the theme of new matter)

The uranium plate in every recipe is the shared "irradiating" element; the chips are useless without the Incubator and vice versa. The mutation recipe type is. Each JSON describes: 2 inputs (fuel + item to mutate) → 1 output + a mutation kind + an optional chance (override). Three mutation kinds (the chip modes):

kindWhat it doesExample (from the MVP)

One item may have recipes in several modes (a diamond: duplicate ×2 and create → irradiated diamond; lapis: transform → redstone and create → mutagen dust). What actually happens is decided by the inserted chip — the machine never picks for the player.

The Duplicate mode — four value classes

The cost of an attempt is fixed (⅓ of an ingot + 4000 EU/FE + 25 s) and does not depend on the item's value, so the only balance lever inside the mode is the success chance: the more valuable the item, the lower the chance. Classes set the default chance; chance is written explicitly in JSON for every class except gems (which use the shared mutationChanceDuplicate default).

ClassChanceItems (25 recipes)
Gems and precious ingots0.45diamond, emerald, amethyst_shard, gold_ingot, silver_ingot, nickel_ingot
Minerals and common ingots0.55coal, redstone, lapis_lazuli, quartz, glowstone_dust, iron_ingot, copper_ingot, tin_ingot

The cheap classes are deliberately "training wheels". Spending rare uranium on seeds is not profitable (⅓ of an ingot ≈ ⅙ of a uranium ore for +0.85 of a seed) — and that is fine: they give the player a cheap way to learn the mechanic and catch their first grades and advancements without risking a diamond. The mode's real economic value lies in gems. Batch processing of cheap items (16 → 32 per attempt) was considered and rejected as needless complexity.

Ingots yes, processed parts no. The line runs between "raw material" and "product": Duplicated: vanilla and mod ingots — iron, copper, gold, tin, silver, nickel. These are base materials, a natural entry point for a miner; the mod's rare ores (silver, nickel) gain a legal way to top up after an unlucky world generation. Not duplicated: everything processed — dusts, plates, machine casings, electronic circuits, chips, cables, as well as raw ore and ore blocks. Reason: the mod already doubles raw material through the macerator (ore → 2 dusts); duplicating the processed products on top of that would devalue the whole crafting chain. Processed parts are cheaper to assemble from duplicated ingots. Uranium is not duplicated — there is no recipe and none is needed: the operation is unprofitable by construction (EV 0.45 + gene ≤ 0.60 < 1 ingot, while ⅓ of an ingot is burnt as fuel).

The Transform mode — a horizontal exchange

The principle: like is traded for like. Transformation never turns cheap into expensive — it grants logistical freedom: getting what your biome or world lacks without mounting an expedition. Upgrading by value (iron → gold, coal → diamond) is forbidden — it would kill the point of exploring and mining. All 27 recipes share the 0.75 chance, a 300-tick duration and a 2400 EU/FE price. The choice problem and its solution — rings. The chip picks the mode, not a specific recipe, so within one mode an item cannot have two recipes. For families with many members (saplings, flowers, soils) this is solved with a ring chain: each item turns into the next one around the circle. Need a particular variant — spin the ring until you reach it. That costs more than a single step, yet still less than a trip across half the world, and it keeps the mechanic deterministic. Minerals (6 recipes) — trading working resources:

inputoutputReverse
lapis_lazuli×1 redstoneyes, 1:1
quartz×1 glowstone_dustyes, 1:1
coal×1 charcoalyes, 1:1

Saplings (8 recipes) — a ring: oak → birch → spruce → jungle → acacia → dark oak → cherry → mangrove propagule → oak. Solves the common pain of "the tree I need is nowhere near my base". Flowers (8 recipes) — a ring: dandelion → poppy → blue orchid → allium → azure bluet → cornflower → lily of the valley → oxeye daisy → dandelion. Access to any dye without hunting biomes. Soils (5 recipes) — a ring dirt → podzol → mycelium → dirt (3) and the pair sand ↔ red sand (2). Terraforming and decoration without long expeditions.

Duplication check. Every transform step is strictly 1:1, the quartz ↔ glowstone dust pair included: no recipe of this mode yields more than one item. It could not be otherwise — an attempt takes exactly one item off the input, so the machine has no way to ask for more of it back, and any multiplier on the output would immediately turn a closed ring into an endless source of material. On top of that the 0.75 chance burns a quarter of the material at every step: a two-step ring returns 0.5625 of what went in, a three-step one 0.42, an eight-step one 0.10. "Spinning for profit" cannot work in principle. Side effect of coal ↔ charcoal: charcoal is renewable, which makes coal renewable too. The two are already interchangeable in practice (fuel, torches) — the only difference is the coal block — so this was deemed acceptable.

The Create mode — evolution ladders

The Incubator's most expensive and most interesting mechanic: an ordinary item evolves into a unique mod material, and that one evolves further along a three-tier ladder. The higher the tier, the lower the chance and the costlier the operation; on upper tiers the player already risks a rare item, so the failure rules there are gentler. Four evolution branches:

BranchTier 1 (from vanilla)Tier 2Tier 3
Diamond — energy densitydiamond → irradiated diamond→ diamond matrix→ singularity core
Crystal — opticsamethyst_shard → resonant shard→ focusing lens→ prismatic core
Biological — agriculturelapis_lazuli → mutagen dust→ living mutagen→ gene matrix
Nuclear — fueldepleted_uranium → unstable isotope→ enriched core→ isotope concentrate
The nuclear branch starts not from a vanilla item but from the Incubator's own ash: the spent uranium piling up from every operation stops being waste and becomes the entry to a chain.

Cost and risk per tier:

Why failure is gentle on tiers 2–3. The input there is already an expensive item earned through 8000 EU/FE and a streak of misses. Losing it outright would mean no player ever risks climbing past tier 1. So from tier 2 onward only the irradiation charge and the energy burn, while the item stays in the slot — you can try again. The exception is slag: 5 % of attempts turn the input into irradiated_slag on any tier, upper ones included. Even a gentle failure has a price. The maths of tier 3: an 8 % chance with no input loss → about 12–13 attempts and roughly 200 000 EU/FE per top-tier item. A genuine endgame grind, but without the risk of losing what you have accumulated. The payoff — machine upgrade chips. Upper-tier materials are not dead weight: they craft into chips for the already existing upgrade slots of machines (4 per machine, the infrastructure is in place — see the mute chip): speed, energy efficiency, a larger buffer, and a "luck chip" for the Incubator itself. The loop closes inside the mod.

Implementation order. Only tier 1 ships in the first iteration — four new materials, one per branch. Tiers 2–3 and the upgrade chips are deferred to a separate task: 8 items + 4 chips is a scope comparable to the Incubator itself. The recipe format needs no changes: the energy and chance fields already express any tier in a single JSON.

56 mutation recipes in total (25 duplicate + 27 transform + 4 create) plus 3 chip crafts. The overlaps between modes are deliberate — they are what justifies the chip system: lapis is available in all three modes (duplicate / turn into redstone / create mutagen dust), diamond in two (duplicate / create an irradiated diamond), quartz in two (duplicate / turn into glowstone dust). The same item yields a different result depending on the chip you insert. What never mutates. The system is a white-list: only items with a JSON recipe mutate. Everything else is simply rejected by the input slot — so duplicating items with contents (shulker boxes, bundles) or with durability/enchantments (tools, armor, enchanted books) is impossible by construction, not by prohibition. A rule for recipe authors: inputs are declared with explicit IDs, never broad tags (#c:ingots would drag in other mods' items). The list below is what we do not write recipes for, even when expanding:

Black-list (explicit): netherite_scrap / netherite_ingot / ancient_debris / dragon_breath / end_crystal / dragon_egg / shulker_shell / elytra / totem_of_undying / enchanted_book — they break vanilla endgame progression or are unique. Plus anything carrying durability, contents or unique components.

The result is taken clean from the recipe (ItemStackTemplate): a diamond renamed on an anvil duplicates into a plain, unnamed diamond.

Progression

Tier: LV. Best built after the first machines (macerator/extractor), once you have a stable uranium vein and an EU/FE grid. Introduces three new resources: (spent uranium) — settles in the ash slot every attempt; a feedstock for the future nuclear phase (MOX/isotopes/RTG, see nuclear/index). — a feedstock for future agriculture/tech recipes. — the biological branch, tier 1. — the crystal branch, tier 1. — the nuclear branch, tier 1; it makes the Incubator's own ash useful. — a by-product of 5 % of attempts; no consumers in the MVP, it simply accumulates as groundwork for future reprocessing (smelting, building blocks, the nuclear phase). Junk with a promise, not dead weight. Introduces three new module items: mutation_chip_duplicate / _transform / _create — crafted from an empty chip, they select the machine's mode. Grade "genes" on materials — groundwork: future consumers (anomalous uranium in the nuclear phase, legendary seeds in agriculture machines) will read mutation_grade in their recipes and bonuses. The gene already matters in this release: a colored name, a lore line, and a bonus to the item's further mutation chances (+5 / +10 / +15 p.p. for Rare / Epic / Legendary). A mutation advancement branch: first mutation, first create, first Legendary outcome. Second iteration: the enhance mode (affixes for powered items) is added on top of this block with no world migration — a new kind, a fourth chip, a new component; existing Incubators simply gain another mode. Does not evolve within v0.1 — an MV version of the Incubator is not planned.

Compatibility

JEI/REI/EMI: its own mutation category with two inputs (fuel + item) and a success-chance indicator; chance-rendering details — see "Open questions". Plus an info page about rarity grades (modelled on the solar-evolution category). Item tooltip: a "Mutation grade: …" line — via the shared dual-loader tooltip channel. Jade/The One Probe: cycle progress, EU/FE storage, mutation mode, multiblock status (formed/broken), ash-slot fill. Automation: hoppers/pipes work with the base (5 slots: chip — push only, fuel — push, input — push, result — pull, ash — pull); the dome is a purely structural block with no inventory. No face lets automation pull the chip: any drain hung on the machine walked off with it and the incubator stalled in silence. The price is that the mode can no longer be switched by automation — it is a build-time choice. An item pipe may return the result into the same machine's input, which is how repeat processing is automated.

Duplication economy (sanity check): 1 uranium ore → macerator → 2 dust → smelter → 2 ingots = 6 attempts. A diamond duplicate: EV +0.45/attempt → +1.35 diamond per ingot, +2.7 per ore. Uranium is ×7 rarer than diamond, so raw diamond mining is still more efficient — the Incubator earns its keep through passivity (it works while you mine), automation and grades, not raw speed. An emerald duplicate: 1.35/ingot versus 0.5/ingot from the trader (2 uranium → 1 emerald) — the machine beats the trade ×2.7; the trade remains the early, deterministic outlet before an EU/FE grid exists. Conclusion: uranium is the real limiter; an auto-farm does not break vanilla progression. Rarity economy (sanity check): the grade changes only quality, never quantity — the material expected value does not grow (the input gene burns and the result is rolled fresh, so Legendaries cannot be multiplied). A Legendary outcome on a diamond duplicate: 0.45 × 0.02 ≈ 1 in ~110 attempts ≈ 37 ingots ≈ 19 uranium ores — genuine "booster pack" rarity on top of a ×7-rare ore.

Open questions

Exact sounds for start/success/failure (ModSounds) — original recordings needed (rule 3 of AGENTS.md), to be decided before spec. Plus a dedicated "jackpot" sound for a Legendary outcome. The exact dome model — silhouette (a tapering octagon? a ribbed hemisphere?), palette and frame thickness. A sketch/reference is needed before spec. Tinting is no longer part of this question: the glass faces of the dome model carry tintindex: 0 and take the dye color of the glass remembered in the base, while the frame and the spire keep the casing's dark metal. Base decoration — what exactly the draws over the cube (emitter ring, spin, glow) and where the "block model vs render" line falls. Before spec. Rendering the item inside the dome — resolving ItemStack → ItemStackRenderState from a has no precedent in the codebase; verify the 26.2 signatures before spec. Replacing the glass with the dome — confirm that remembering the original glass in the block entity and returning it on dismantling does not clash with vanilla drops; check explosions, pistons and lava. JEI/REI category — how to render the chance and two inputs (chance number on the result icon?); how to present grades (an info page vs percentages in every recipe) and modes. Idle uranium_ingot → depleted_uranium recipe — redundant under the "ash" model (depleted already drips from every operation). Decision: do not add; revisit only if a dedicated ash farm is ever needed. lifecycle_status: concept → spec — the core design forks (probabilistic model, 5 slots, chip-selected modes, local multiblock, MVP recipes, 7 new items) are resolved; the move to spec follows once the sound and the dome model are fixed.
